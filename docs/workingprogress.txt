DRAGONGEN DEVELOPMENT ROADMAP
Last reorganized: 4 October 2026

======================================================================
1. CORE VISION
======================================================================

The tribe should remember.

Actions should create relationships.
Relationships should create stories.
Stories should create future consequences.

DragonGen is a social simulation and procedural story generator. Its central
goal is to create believable dragons with flaws, preferences, social status,
relationships, histories, and evolving character arcs inside a living tribe.

The player should remember dragons because of what they did and whom they
affected—not merely because of their statistics.

Immediate effects are good.
Delayed effects are better.
Remembered effects are best.

Persistent social links may include:

- Parents, grandparents, and siblings
- Caretakers and wards
- Mentors and apprentices
- Friends and rivals
- Mates and former mates
- First hunt or patrol partners
- Political allies and enemies
- Dragons connected through injuries, rescues, betrayals, and deaths


======================================================================
2. TRIBE DIFFERENTIATION — CORE DESIGN PILLAR
======================================================================

Every tribe must share DragonGen's underlying simulation principles while
creating a meaningfully different campaign. Tribe identity cannot be limited
to colour palettes, name pools, background art, or small statistical bonuses.

SHARED FOUNDATION

Every tribe uses the same core systems:

- Aging, injury, death, reproduction, and lineage
- Personality, mood, relationships, memory, and reputation
- Food, hunting, patrols, training, healing, and leadership
- Locations, weekly events, player choices, and long-term consequences
- Individual histories, family records, biographies, and obituaries

LAYERS OF TRIBE IDENTITY

Each tribe should differ through at least five interconnected layers:

1. Social structure — family groups, hierarchy, leadership, and belonging
2. Cultural values — which traits and actions earn prestige or disapproval
3. Biological traits — common abilities, rare conditions, and life stages
4. Locations and events — tribe-specific activities, conflicts, and choices
5. Language and presentation — titles, terminology, UI motifs, and narration

A tribe-specific mechanic is substantial only when it affects at least three
systems. For example, a MudWing sibling group should affect relationships,
location behaviour, event choices, grief, and individual history—not merely
appear as a family-tree label.

IMPLEMENTATION STRATEGY

Do not build ten separate simulations or scatter dozens of
"if tribe == ..." checks throughout the code.

[ ] Create a data-driven Tribe Identity/Profile structure
[ ] Give every tribe standard hooks for family, prestige, rare traits, events,
    locations, role biases, dialogue, and history text
[ ] Build MudWings first as the complete vertical slice
[ ] Test whether a MudWing campaign genuinely feels different by week 10
[ ] Generalize the successful framework before implementing the next tribe
[ ] Add tribes incrementally and test each one independently
[ ] Store culture and birth-condition data in save files
[ ] Allow mixed tribes to inherit traits without automatically receiving every
    advantage from both parents

[ ] Make sibling bonds unusually strong and durable

MIXED TRIBES

[ ] Avoid making mixed dragons automatically stronger than single-tribe dragons
[ ] Allow upbringing to affect culture independently from biological inheritance

RARE POWERS AND CAMPAIGN ERA

Rare powers must be world-changing, not decorative bonuses.

[ ] Base animus potential on ancestry and campaign settings, not a flat random roll
[ ] Begin with animus ancestry or latent potential before implementing free-form magic
[ ] Design costs, secrecy, fear, political pressure, and long-term consequences
[ ] Define a campaign era because arenas, royal systems, mind control, wars, and
    access to animus magic are not equally appropriate in every historical period
[ ] Keep rare traits rare enough that their appearance creates a memorable campaign

3. CURRENT DEVELOPMENT PRIORITY
======================================================================

CURRENT MILESTONE: STABILIZATION AND SYSTEM INTEGRATION

The interface and primary locations have received major upgrades. The next
step is not another large redesign. The next step is proving that the existing
systems remain coherent across a long campaign.

Recommended order:

1. Conduct a deliberate 25-40 week playtest.
2. Record confirmed bugs separately from uncertain or outdated notes.
3. Verify save/load after major events and with pending choices.
4. Inspect whether roles, skills, relationships, injuries, rumors, food, and
   history influence one another as intended.
5. Repair broken links between existing systems.
6. Build role progression and cross-system consequences.
7. Build the MudWing tribe-identity vertical slice.
8. Generalize the tribe framework and add other tribes incrementally.
9. Expand politics, resources, and visual polish only after stabilization.

4. PLAYTEST CHECKLIST
======================================================================

Run at least one continuous 25-40 week campaign. Do not immediately repair
every minor issue while testing; record it first so broader patterns become
visible.

SAVE AND STATE

[ ] New game creates a valid tribe and roster
[ ] Fixed save slots work correctly
[ ] Loading restores the correct week, food, population, and locations
[ ] Pending choices remain pending after save/load
[ ] Resolved choices do not reappear after save/load
[ ] Returning to the main menu offers the correct save options
[ ] Restarting or replacing a tribe creates a valid roster

POPULATION AND LIFE CYCLE

[ ] Population remains stable enough to sustain a long campaign
[ ] Reproduction is selective and not excessively frequent
[ ] Dragonets cannot receive adult roles, mates, rivals, or patrol assignments
[ ] Dragons age and die within the intended lifespan
[ ] Death, parentage, mates, and descendants remain correctly recorded
[ ] Injured dragons recover, worsen, or die according to understandable rules

ROLES AND LOCATIONS

[ ] Hunters prefer the Hunting Grounds without emptying other locations
[ ] Scouts prefer Border Routes without emptying other locations
[ ] Healers perform meaningful healing work
[ ] Warriors train and improve appropriately

ECONOMY AND RISK

[ ] Food rises and falls at a believable rate
[ ] Hunts remaining reset correctly each week
[ ] Patrols remaining reset correctly each week
[ ] Small, large, and dangerous hunts have distinct risk/reward profiles

SOCIAL SIMULATION

[ ] Relationships change for understandable reasons
[ ] Rumors can be true, false, or unsubstantiated

TRIBE IDENTITY

[ ] The founding tribe changes more than artwork, names, and attribute weights

[ ] Tribe-specific prestige affects reputation, roles, or relationships
[ ] Rare traits obey their required ancestry or birth conditions
[ ] Tribe-specific events create consequences recorded in histories
[ ] Mixed dragons inherit limited traits and culturally plausible identities

PLAYER CHOICES

[ ] Events surface globally but resolve at the correct location
[ ] Choice indicators clearly identify locations needing attention
[ ] Choice outcomes appear in the decision interface
[ ] Outcomes update relationships, reputation, injury, food, or history
[ ] Choices create delayed consequences rather than only immediate text

7. NEXT SYSTEM: ROLE PROGRESSION AND CONSEQUENCES
======================================================================

This is the strongest next gameplay upgrade because it connects systems that
already exist instead of adding another isolated feature.

DESIRED LOOP

Training or work
    -> skill growth
    -> improved hunt, patrol, healing, or leadership performance
    -> success, failure, injury, rescue, or conflict
    -> reputation and relationship changes
    -> remembered history and future opportunities

TRAINING GROUNDS

[ ] Training improves combat skill
[ ] Hunting training improves hunt success
[ ] Scout training improves border survival and discovery
[ ] Leadership training influences future promotion candidates
[ ] Healer training improves treatment outcomes
[ ] Mentorship creates loyalty and relationship memories
[ ] Sparring can cause appropriate minor injuries
[ ] Leaders can order tribe-wide training priorities
[ ] Training history appears on individual dragon profiles

ROLE PROGRESSION

[ ] Define skill ranges and meaningful thresholds
[ ] Add slow improvement through successful work
[ ] Add rare regression through age, severe injury, or prolonged inactivity
[ ] Use experience and reputation in promotion decisions
[ ] Allow exceptional performance to create titles or social recognition
[ ] Ensure personality influences how a dragon performs and develops

13. DOCUMENTATION MAINTENANCE RULES
======================================================================

When adding a note, place it in exactly one of these categories:

- CONFIRMED BUG: reproducible and currently broken
- NEEDS VERIFICATION: suspected, old, or uncertain
- NEXT SYSTEM: intentionally selected for near-term development
- SYSTEM BACKLOG: valuable but not currently selected
- VISUAL POLISH: presentation-only work
- COMPLETED: implemented and tested
- LATER IDEA: interesting but not yet justified

When something is completed, move it rather than duplicating it. Add a brief
note only when the implementation differs materially from the original idea.

The old file-map notes are intentionally omitted because the architecture has
changed substantially. Refresh the project file map through an actual code
review rather than preserving obsolete descriptions.
